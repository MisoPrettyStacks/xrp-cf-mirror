How it works

Every 5 minutes: fresh 5-minute candles → 21 features (volatility-scaled reversals, 4-hour regime, candle shape, clock-time) → LightGBM + logistic regression blend → isotonic calibration → shrinkage toward 50%.

Labels come from 6,427 settled 15-minute windows of free public market history — the same settlement feed the windows resolve on. No order-book data, no paid feeds.

A 15-minute crypto direction model built from free data should be expected to land within a point or two of 50%. This one is honest about that — and the live ledger below proves it.

Methodology, mathematics and theory

0. What is being forecast

Let $t$ index 15-minute windows opening at :00, :15, :30, :45 UTC. Each window has a reference price $K_t$ (the settlement feed's value at the open) and a closing value $C_t$ at the end of the window. The outcome is $$y_t = \mathbf{1}[C_t \geq K_t] \in \{0,1\},$$ i.e. 1 if the window closes at or above its reference ("up"), 0 otherwise. The forecast is $$p_t = \Pr(y_t = 1 \mid \mathcal{F}_t),$$ where $\mathcal{F}_t$ contains only information available at the window open — 5-minute candles that had fully closed, nothing more.

Accuracy is judged with strictly proper scoring rules (Brier 1950; Gneiting & Raftery 2007): a forecaster maximizes its expected score only by reporting its true belief. The no-skill reference is $p = \tfrac{1}{2}$ with Brier score $0.25$. The design goal: be calibrated first, extract whatever small real skill exists second, and never be more confident than the live record justifies.

1. Data

  • Labels: 6,427 settled 15-minute windows (Jul 31 – Oct 7, 2026) from free public market history — each with its reference (strike), outcome, and timestamps. This is the settlement truth the model is measured against.
  • Features: 5-minute XRP-USD candles from the free Coinbase Exchange public API (no key). 20,441 bars covering the full label period plus warmup.
  • Alignment: Coinbase candle timestamps mark the start of each 5-minute bucket. Features for window $t$ use the last fully-closed bar before the window open — using the bar at the open would leak the window's first 5 minutes. This exact bug was caught during development (it briefly showed a fraudulent AUC of 0.79) and fixed before anything went live.

2. Features (21, all dimensionless)

Volatility is an exponentially weighted moving average of squared 5-minute log returns $r_i = \ln(P_i/P_{i-1})$ (RiskMetrics, J.P. Morgan 1996): $$\sigma_i^2 = \lambda\sigma_{i-1}^2 + (1-\lambda)r_i^2, \qquad \lambda = 0.94.$$

Volatility-scaled returns at six horizons: $$z_{k} = \frac{\ln(P_i/P_{i-k})}{\sigma_i\sqrt{k}}, \qquad k \in \{1,3,12,36,144,288\}.$$ Scaling by volatility is what makes short-horizon features work — a 10 bp move means nothing in a storm and everything in calm water (Wiley, Financial Review: realized-vol scaling preserves the jump and order-flow bursts that carry signal).

Oscillators at 15-minute and 4-hour resamples — RSI(14), Bollinger %B(20,2) — because the literature finds 4-hour regime features carry roughly a third of total signal (Sobreiro et al. 2026, Forecasting). Volatility and volume microstructure: Parkinson's estimator $$\hat\sigma_P^2 = \frac{1}{20 \cdot 4\ln 2}\sum_{j=i-19}^{i}\ln^2\!\left(\frac{H_j}{L_j}\right),$$ volume z-score vs. the 1-day rolling median, candle body ratio $\frac{|C-O|}{H-L}$, and upper/lower wick ratios (proxies for intrabar aggression). Clock-time: quarter-hour-of-hour, hour-of-day, day-of-week, weekend flag — crypto shows periodic algo-driven bursts at clock marks (Kim & Hansen 2026). A conditional reversal term $-\mathrm{sign}(z_3)\max(0,|z_3|-1.5)$ fades only large moves, per Kitron & Wengrowicz (2026).

3. The two models

LightGBM — gradient-boosted decision trees on binary log-loss, deliberately small (15 leaves, minimum 200 samples per leaf, feature/bagging fractions 0.7, L2 $\lambda = 5$). Small trees with heavy regularization because the signal is faint and overfitting is the default outcome.

Logistic regression — $p = \sigma(w^\top x)$, L2-penalized ($C = 0.5$), on standardized features. Kept because plain linear boundaries generalize best across regime shifts (Sobreiro et al.: LogReg beat XGBoost/LightGBM on the 2025 holdout).

The two are blended evenly: $$p_{\text{blend}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,p_{\text{lgbm}} + \tfrac{1}{2}\,p_{\text{lr}}.$$

4. Calibration and shrinkage

Raw model outputs are recalibrated with isotonic regression fit on walk-forward out-of-fold predictions — a non-parametric map from raw scores to empirical frequencies. Then the shrinkage guard: $$p_t = \tfrac{1}{2} + \lambda_s\,(\tilde p_t - \tfrac{1}{2}), \qquad \lambda_s = 0.5,$$ pulling every forecast halfway back to 50%. With Brier skill in the tenths of a percent, humility is optimal: the model must earn wider confidence with roughly a thousand scored forecasts.

5. Walk-forward validation (the honest scoreboard)

Five folds, expanding training window, 7-day test blocks, 1-day embargo between train and test (purged/embargoed CV is mandatory with overlapping 15-minute labels — random splits leak across the horizon and inflate everything). Results on 3,360 out-of-sample windows:

LightGBM0.53953.2%−1.1%
Logistic regression0.53853.2%+0.0%

This sits at the top of the literature's credible range (AUC 0.52–0.55 for free 15-min crypto data). Anything claiming much more is overfit, a different target, or for sale.

6. The self-improvement system

Champion/challenger. Every Sunday the system refreshes its data and trains a challenger. The challenger is promoted only if its walk-forward Brier skill beats the champion's by a real margin ($> 0.05$pp, not noise). Otherwise the champion stands and the decision is logged.

Research agent. Every Monday it scans arXiv (q-fin), SSRN, Ivy League repositories, and PhD theses for new work on short-horizon crypto predictability. Promising ideas are implemented and backtested; only winners survive — a volume-gated reversal term from Kitron & Wengrowicz was tested and discarded when it failed to beat the champion out-of-sample.

Live ledger. Every 5-minute forecast is logged with its window and probability; a scorer matches them against settlements and publishes rolling accuracy, AUC, Brier skill, and calibration bins. The page's stats section is computed from this ledger — never back-filled.

7. Limits and honest expectations

  • A 15-minute crypto direction forecast from free price/volume data should be expected to deliver Brier skill of at most a few percent and a hit rate within a point or two of 50%. If the live record shows no skill, the page says so.
  • The scores concern probabilistic accuracy, not trading profit; fees, spread, and slippage are ignored — and at ~1 bp of gross edge per bet vs. 5–20 bp round-trip costs, spot trading on this signal loses money. The prediction-market framing is the only one where the cost math works.
  • With AUC ≈ 0.55, honest probabilities live in roughly $[0.44, 0.65]$. That looks unimpressive and it is the truth. Widening the range would be a calibration failure.
  • GitHub Actions schedules are best-effort: a missed window is a gap in the ledger, never an invented forecast.
